- **Description**: The new multiple-entity paragraph overstates what a subsection provides. It says a subsection with section.focus makes each related entity's information "independently locatable, attestable, and queryable."

"Independently attestable" seems especially problematic: there is no section.attester; Composition.attester attests the Composition/document. The specification itself says the attester attests the document, subject resource, and resources referenced from section entries. Similarly, there is no standard search parameter shown for Composition.section.focus, so "independently queryable" is at least stronger than the base specification guarantees.

In addition, the existing text example about Family History, stating the subjection pattern improves queriability etc. is not true for 2 reasons:

1) The Family History resource provides the identification of the family member at FamilyMemberHistory.relationship which is 1..1. Therefore if a Family History Resource is present placing information about each Family Member in a seperate section is unecessary, redundant and could be recursive.

2) If entries are not used and family members' conditions or other information are conveyed only in a subsection's section.text, interoperability and queryability are not inherently improved. Achieving those benefits would require more specific profiling in an implementation guide, including constraints or expectations for the use of section.focus.

The use of subsections for the stated purposes should be discouraged and limited to cases where they are necessary to support readability when the Composition is rendered.

## Comments

### 1. John Moehrke - 2026-09-15

I brought up your position on Permission is not in the core with FHIR-I and got strong pushback from Lloyd and Grahame. https://chat.fhir.org/#narrow/channel/179280-fhir.2Finfrastructure-wg/topic/incubators.20real.20or.20not.3F/with/624352982

Those that are using Consent without a patient understood that they were on borrowed time. Now is the time to make these kinds of changes.

### 2. Mohammad Jafari - 2026-09-14

I think for two reasons I disagree with this decision: (a) Permission is not in the core. If it were in the core I think this would be reasonable. (b) this will create unnecessary breaking change that could pain older versions of FHIR consent to be imported to the future version.

I think instead of a hard constraints we can add language to strongly discourage the use of consent without a subject for modelling generic access control policy and recommend the use of incubator resource Permission for this purpose if it is available in the implementer's FHIR server.

### 3. John Moehrke - 2026-06-08

These use-cases for Permission can be brought to Permission where justified elements can easily be added. Consent has a clear scope that is patient specific and Permission is the solution for non-patient specific use-cases. (Further if we did make the change as Hans is discussing, then the resource would not be appropriate to call Consent and thus it would need to be renamed. The renaming would move the resource out of Normative (R6) track.)

### 4. Hans Buitendijk - 2026-06-04

Considering that Consent has relevant meta data that also are relevant to jurisdictional privacy rules, the Permission Resource could possibly replace Consent.provision, but would require the addition of various metadata such as cateogory, period, manager, controller, sourceAttachment, sourceReference, policyBasis, and policyText. It therefore seems that profiling Consent to either use case (patient consent and jurisidiction rules) would be more reasonable to maintain consistency in concept and format of these different, but very similar rules as to who can access what and why.

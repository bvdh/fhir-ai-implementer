- **Description**: Need a way other than terminology (which is extensible- i.e., not required) to indicate that a condition is absent, but has a clinical significance to the patient. This is common to record if there are conditions that are absent in the patient which may directly inform the clinical workflow (i.e., the assessment and treatment planning for the patient).

For example:

Absence of Hormone Receptors (e.g., Estrogen, Progesterone)
Absence of Genetic Mutations (e.g., BRCA1, BRCA2)
Absence of Allergies - esp. in context of known allergies (e.g., Allergy to Tree Nuts, but No Allergy to Peanuts)

Additional Context and Examples:

Pertinent Positive:

Diabetic retinopathy

Pertinent Negatives important in association with diabetic retinopathy:

No Vitreous hemorrhage
No glaucoma
No vision loss
No neuropathy
No nephropathy
No cardiovascular conditions
Employment: No employment is an observation that could also be a problem.

These are examples of pertinent negatives. They are pertinent negatives to exclude in the differential diagnosis to rule out an acute situation requiring immediate intervention. Having negation included in the terminology as one statement indicates it to be a condition or status, such as 'unemployed', which it should not. Employment is an observation that could also be a problem.

Having negation represented with consistency is key for interoperability as complexity (reduced pre-combinatorial explosion) is minimized and simplicity optimized. This mitigates the potential for errors and is computable for machine processing (& learning), allowing for modularity, reusability, inferencing, CDS and research. Regarding research, iso-semantics, or equivalence, needs to be determined with near 100% certainty for CDS and other use cases.

++

In FHIR, there is inconsistent representation of negation. As discussed above, the increases complexity. For example:

In the conditions resource, negation is represented by Condition.clinicalStatus: active | recurrence | relapse | inactive | remission | resolved
In the observations resource, negation is represented by Observation.component.valueCodeableConcept: 'Negative'
In the familyMemberHistory resource, negation is represented by FamilyMemberHistory.condition.code: "No Known Problems" or negated conditions, such as "No history of malignant tumor of breast", for an individual family member.
In the AllergyIntolerance resource, negation is represented by AllergyIntolerance.verificationStatus: unconfirmed | confirmed | refuted | entered-in-error

As can be seen, while similar to some degree, these FHIR Resources are inconsistent in their representation and subject to the risks noted above.

### 9. Jay Lyle - 2023-01-30

One perspective:

First, as always, FHIR is not about process reengineering; it's an interoperability specification. As such, it must support communication among a variety of stakeholders of varying levels of sophistication. Where we wish to support prospective process improvement, we can only do so with designs that continue to support existing stakeholders as unambiguously as possible. Creating additional designs (not standards, yet) that leverage those specifications for more sophisticated purposes and better care may be a great path forward for standards in the future. See FHIR Principles.

Second, we see an opportunity to harmonize the representation of absence in FamilyHistory.

Third, FHIR supports most of these requirements as it stands.

A record of a condition is a record of concern, not of absence of concern. It's like a bank account: you don't create a record unless you're creating an account.

AllergyIntolerance is a safety tool: it's used for order checks. Negatives are transient and not persistently useful except for process audit, and are typically handled with an "NKA" code (which is why FHIR does so as well).
A recorded negative can also help avoid repetetive questioning in a single encounter, but text supports this case: the fact that this information is not useful for order checks may be why there are so few substance-specific absence codes.

Observation is actually a meta-model, and its coded nature supports cases such as queries about the presence or absence of phenomena. This is the place to record pertinent negatives (no glaucoma, no BRCA1).

Family History is under review. It currently seems to align with the Allergy pattern, but it may be better served by the Observation pattern.

The example "Absence of hormone receptors" raises a question.

Previous examples of absences of concern (e.g., "absence of stable income") are easily represented with "problem" terms, e.g., "low income," "variable income," "insufficient income," just as absence of spleen would typically be called "asplenia." Cases where no sufficiently precise condition code exists can use more general condition codes supported by more precise text or pertinent negative Observations. The option of coordinating negative semantics in Condition seems to introduce unwarranted complexity in these cases, because in cases where some deficiency causes a known problem, there tends to be a word for it.

The genomic and proteomic domains introduce a large number of new kinds of physiologic sufficiency that might seem to warrant explicit absence representations as Conditions.

One solution would be to add a post-coordinated absence dimension to the Condition resource, with objective and repeatable guidance around semantic limits on the kinds of things you're allowed to postcoordinate and what to do when communicating with partners who don't support that capability. See CDA for some examples of the kinds of issues you'd need to solve.

Another would be, as above, to use a Concern ("Hormone receptor deficiency") or a more general Concern (e.g., hereditary disease) associated with a pertinent negative Observation ("Hormone receptors" + "absent"). This would not introduce additional needs for semantic clarification of the Condition resource.

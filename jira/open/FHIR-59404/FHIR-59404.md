- **Description**: The definitions of Practitioner and RelatedPerson are tricky when care is being provided in and by the home/family unit, rather than by an institution.

1. The Practitioner vs RelatedPerson boundary is defined by the care-delivery organization. The boundary text says it depends on whether the person "operates on behalf of the care delivery organization" (Practitioner) or is "not associated with the organization" (RelatedPerson). Practitioner also says it "SHALL NOT be used for persons involved without a formal responsibility". This breaks down where there's no organization:

a self-employed support worker paid directly by the family, as under consumer-directed schemes like the NDIS
a volunteer who helps several people
a patient-held or family-run record

The RelatedPerson intro frames it from the institution's side too: it's for someone "documented within the context of a patient's chart", with the example of "a neighbor bringing a patient to the hospital".

2. RelatedPerson is always relative to a single patient. RelatedPerson.patient is 1..1, so a carer isn't an actor in their own right:

A parent caring for three children, or someone caring for both of their parents, is several RelatedPerson resources.
The only thing tying them together is Person, which has little support elsewhere and doesn't appear as a reference target in actor roles.
A carer can't author something that spans the people they care for, or be a stable identity across a family's care.

The new PersonalRelationship resource helps with discovering relationships, but its boundary text says explicitly that it "cannot do things". For institutions this is fine: the hospital sees one patient's chart at a time. It's awkward for the carer's own view of the world.

I'm asking PA to review the definitions and design in the light of home/family based care considerations

## Comments

### 1. Cooper Thompson - 2026-10-06

PA has generally held the line that in the context of FHIR, the Patient resource is the correct resource to represent a recipient of services, even if the term "patient" isn't the appropriate term for end users in a given context. This has come up often in the context of payment, where the terms "member" or "beneficiary" are the common terms used. Regardless of whether the recipient is known as a member, beneficiary, patient, or something else, the "Patient" resource is the appropriate resource to represent a person receiving services in FHIR. System can choose to display whatever term they like to their end users, since "Patient" is just the name of the data type on the wire.

This is addressed directly here: https://build.fhir.org/patient.html#8.1.2

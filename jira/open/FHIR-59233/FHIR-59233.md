### 3. Michelle Miller - 2026-09-22

WGM - Tues Q4

A given reaction can have multiple manifestations (e.g. anaphylaxis can include wheezing hives, itching)

Some reactions are predictable (manifestations can appear and disappear throughout the time period) - may not all be captured.

Manifestations are not unique to allergies - could occur from other disease processes. Misdiagnosis of allergy (hives from viral infections in children are normal and might be taking antibiotics).

Amit said that what Russ calls "manifestation" is what others call "reactions" - Amit supports the compromise approach (in spreadsheet view attached). EHRs call it "reactions"

Immunization > ache > fever > rash – are these manifestations to immunization administration? This would be one reaction event (one episode of the administration) with multiple manifestations.

There could be documentation of a sign/symptom before the association (cause/effect identified) with the allergy or immunization. There will be a date/time with the observed manifestation (does it need to be in allergy record?)

EHRs lump allergy and intolerances together in the same module/resource. Some manifestations are predictable. Ask to separate allergies and intolerances, but out of scope for this JIRA.

Observation.context can be used to reference Condition which has sverity.

Do we need an exposureDate added?

onset - Estimated or actual date, date-time, or age when allergy or intolerance was identified
recordedDate - The recordedDate represents when this particular AllergyIntolerance record was created in the system, which is often a system-generated date.
lastOccurrence - Represents the date and/or time of the last known occurrence of a reaction event
reaction.onset - Record of the date and/or time of the onset of the Reaction

Grahame said that the exposure date isn't commonly there, but some do have it (in notes/documentation)

Majority of allergies are reported by patients (so exposure date isn't typically documented).

If, during administration, it will be more clearly documented.

Drafted Resolution
If there are multiple "reaction" episodes (exposures) and each manifestation has a different onset date, then MUST use Observation (since each Observation has its own onset date). Can't get to multiple onset dates if using CodeableConcept because there is only one onset per reaction.

Amit to move as Not Persuasive - documentation is most often patient/related person reported and onset date is already there (patients/related doesn't specify exposure date)

### 5. Amit Popat - 2026-09-22

In general, what we document as a "Reaction" or list of "Reactions" is really a list of manifestations of that single reaction. However, to keep things simple, that when clinicians document reactions in Epic, they are most probably manifestations of the reaction to the event. I would recommend that we keep it simple and not introduce both reactions and manifestations in the mix as that would probably lead to more confusion than necessary.

### 6. Cooper Thompson - 2026-09-16

To provide one data point, in Epic, we only track a (discrete) list of reactions for an allergy. This list would just be like "hives, diarrhea, dry mouth". We do not track specific manifestations in the Allergy.

With the caveat that we do have a large Comments field, so of course these could be documented free text.

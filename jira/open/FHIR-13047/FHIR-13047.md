### 5. David Barwin - 2026-04-07

Just to clarify, the dosing instruction discussion isn’t necessarily specific to blood products. For example, in a cellular therapy context, you might order an overall dose of stemcells (e.g., 2×10^6 CD34+ cells/kg) administered over multiple days, with individual transfusion procedures delivering smaller doses each day. Those daily doses may vary depending on the contents of each product and are clinically significant events.

In this type of use case, it’s not necessarily critical for the Procedure resource itself to carry detailed dosing information, though that remains open for discussion.

In the R4/R5 world, the Procedure.used element has been used to reference the specific BiologicallyDerivedProduct instance administered to the patient. These cellular therapies are medical products of human origin manufactured in a stem cell processing lab, and do not typically have standard medication codes in the way traditional pharmaceuticals do. This creates a practical barrier to using MedicationAdministration, which generally assumes a coded medication concept.

As a short-term approach, introducing a dosing extension on Procedure was thought to help capture the necessary dosing information. Longer term, there is a need for clearer guidance or alignment in the resource modelling strategy across Procedure, MedicationAdministration, and BiologicallyDerivedProduct. OO is hoping to pick this topic up again at the Rotterdam WGM to further plot this out, even if the ultimate solution ends up being profiles on existing resources.

### 12. Paul Ashford - 2022-10-31

My expertise is on the management of blood products through to the Transfusion Laboratory so I am not the best person to comment on the information needs associated with clinical use. However I would make the following comments that may be helpful:

Blood transfusion, and the clinical use of all medical products of human origin, has unique traceability requirements that have to be satisfied. Specifically, the bi-directional traceability from donor to all recipients, and from recipient to donor.

The unique nature of transfusion information requirements was recognized many years ago and resulted in the development of Transfusion Service (Blood Bank) Trigger Events and Messages in HL7 v2. It is stated in the v2 text regarding the Blood Product Dispense Status Message that: "In the pre-transfusion processing of blood products, it is necessary for the transfusion service and the placer system to communicate information that is not included in the current HL7 order/observation model. Examples of pre-transfusion processing include performing a crossmatch test to ensure compatibility with the patient, or irradiation of the blood product due to a special transfusion requirement for the patient. The blood product dispense status messages need to contain additional information regarding the blood products requested, such as the Donation ID, product code, blood type, expiration date/time and current status of the blood product."

I believe there is probably a need to develop an equivalent blood product dispense resource (or more broadly a biologically derived product dispense resource) in FHIR and would recommend bringing hospital transfusion laboratory experts into the discussion.

- **Resolution Description**: We will make the following changes:

In the Notes section called "Recording Body Position" after 10.1.5.9 Physiologically Relevant Time of the Observation:

When it is necessary or required to record the body position for an observation, use Observation.context rather than using Observation.component. Observation.context allows for a reference or a code to represent the position of the body at the time of the. observation. More information is found in Notes.

In the comment for Obseration.context, we will add "This is the preferred element to capture body position at the time of the observation."

### 6. KevinO'Donnell - 2026-07-14

In imaging, the term "body position" covers maybe three things, which are mostly handled at the imaging study level, rather than individual observations.

How the patient is positioned by the tech (prone, supine, left decubitus, standing, sitting, inverse Trendelenburg, etc) is captured in the image headers as Patient Orientation Modifier (codeset CID 20 Patient Orientation Modifier). This gives you information about the direction of gravity which affects how soft tissues shift around and whether joints are weight-bearing or not etc.

How the patient is "presented" to the scanner (headfirst, feetfirst, etc) is captured as the Patient Equipment Relationship (codeset CID 21 Patient Equipment Relationship).

There were some recent updates to the above to accommodate new "standing CT" gantry designs/capabilities. cp2370_ft_VerticalCTGantry (The fun stuff starts on Pg 7).

So mostly this is captured as characteristics of the imaging exam/data and is not re-iterated in each observation.

The one thing that might be at the observation level is the (relatively uncommon case) where aspects of the patient positioning is dynamically changed over the course of a single study acquisition. E.g. taking multiple images of the knee during a single study and changing the angle of the knee joint for each image. In that case it is added to the imaging data (since it is a description of the imaging rather than an observation) as special metadata elements, for example in an Acquisition Context module . TID 3460 Projection Radiography Acquisition Context

In Observation it could be an aspect of the observation context which we put into Observation.context.

short: When request should be performed
definition: The date or time(s) at which the activity or service is desired to occur.
comment: Note that the timing specified in this element is an overall timing for the request; details for individual timings of activities within the request orchestration will be specified in the action or reference activity resource.

and

performer

Definition
Indicates who or what is being asked to perform the request.

Comment:
Note that the performer specified in this request is an overall performer; individual actions will generally identify roles, not specific individuals. However, for the overall order, if it's a directed order, this element represents the organization or healthcare service (or sometimes individual practitioner) who will have responsibility for the whole order. Even in the case that it is not a directed authorization, there'll still be a fulfillment request task sent for the overall request orchestration and that request will typically be directed to a single performer.

- **Description**: When ordering a complex therapy, there's typically a need to indicate when the overall therapy is to start and when the authorization ends. In some cases, the complex regimen itself might repeat. At minimum, need occurrencePeriod. However, date and Timing might also be reasonable.

Similarly, when proposing/authorizing a complex therapy, there's often a need to identify who is being authorized - the practitioner, the organization, the healthcare service, etc.

Look at the Request pattern for both of these.

### 5. Lloyd McKenzie - 2026-09-09

Yes. Every order has an overall "effective" date and "end" date for the authorization. Trying to infer that by looking across all the actions is not workable. In many cases, the detailed activity timings won't specify dates at all, because the timing for each will be dependent on when the process 'starts'. The time period over which the order is valid is a different thing.

In terms of performer, for the individual actions you'll generally identify roles, not specific individuals. However, for the overall order, if it's a directed order, you want to know the organization or healthcare service (or sometimes individual practitioner) who will have responsibility for the whole thing. This is relevant in prior auth, but I'd expect it to be relevant for order routing/acceptance/rejection too. Even if it's not a directed authorization, there'll still be a "please fulfill" task sent for the overall request orchestration and that'll be directed to a single performer.

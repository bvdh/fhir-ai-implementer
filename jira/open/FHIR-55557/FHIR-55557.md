- **Description**: It is unclear why the dragon icon ( used to indicate a warning to the reader) is used with the text:

"For examples and further guidance, it is strongly suggested that an implementer consult the Clinical Care AdverseEvent Implementation Guide and the Clinical Research AdverseEvent Implementation Guide They contain valuable extensions, value sets and, importantly, examples."

It would seem to imply that reading these guides could potentially lead to clinical safety issues. That is not what is meant. I assume the dragons refer an issue that these guides help to resolve.

### 2. Rebecca Baker - 2026-09-17

When the Dragon icon was added, it was meant to serve as a warning that there are differences in the two use cases and considerations in implementing the Adverse Event resource by the use case that implementers should be aware of.

https://build.fhir.org/ig/FHIR/ig-guidance/best-practice.html

The "dragon" while it serves to warn about issues and pitfalls. It is noted to be "This note (styled 'stu-note') contains a warning message. This message may be used to draw the readers' attention to some points, e.g. to request feedback, or be aware of any constraints, or disclaimers." In my experience with FHIR IGs, I have seen this used for this purpose.

### 3. Maryam Garza - 2026-02-10

BRR has reviewed and recommends that the Dragon symbol be replaced by something more appropriate.

The Dragon is often used when there is a level of uncertainty to what has been stated in the text.

The intention of the quoted text referencing the IGs was to stress that the implementation and use of the resource can be a bit tricky and may differ depending on the context (clinical vs. research); and, as such, the reader should reference the IGs to make sure they are implementing correctly.

Perhaps instead of an image, text such as "Important Note" be used preceding the quoted text. Or a different symbol, such as the warning sign: .

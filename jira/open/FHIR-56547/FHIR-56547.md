We think it is ok that non-patient AppointmentResponses are excluded from the compartment. The Appointment is the resource that tracks the ultimate participation.

We note that you've marked this as in-person, so let us know when you are available to discuss further.
- **Request in-person**: Lloyd McKenzie
- **Description**: The only resources reliably tied to a patient compartment are those returned when performing a search on the 'patient' search parameter. As designed right now, only the AppointmentResponse for the patient's participation will be tied to the patient record, even if the appointment is tied to the patient record. This is likely a problem.

I think there needs to be a 'subject' on AppointmentResponse too to ensure patient compartment membership is maintained.

## Comments

### 1. Lloyd McKenzie - 2026-09-23

I would expect the resource to point to the Group in that situation.

There are some resources that are truly subject-independent (Medication, NutritionProduct, Questionnaire, etc.) They can be shared across subjects. However AppointmentResponse is wholly tied to the Appointment and has notes that may have patient-centric information in them. If you strongly feel it needs to live outside the patient compartment, then at minimum, put guidance on the notes along the lines of "The content in this element must be managed to reduce/eliminate the risk of any patient-specific information, as this resource is not directly associated with the patient record."

### 2. Brian Postlethwaite - 2026-09-23

So would all the resources associated with a group, they aren't in the patient compartment (where the patient is a part of a group).

The appointment response is supporting material to the information in the actual appointment. (you don't need to look at a response to know if the participant accepted - it's updated i the appointment)

### 3. Lloyd McKenzie - 2026-09-23

I don't understand the reluctance to expose Patient if the AppointmentResponse is tied to a patient appointment. If you're doing a dump of "all information relevant to this patient", then the responses to a patient's appointments logically would fall within that bucket, and I don't see a downside to making the relationship explicit.

### 4. Cooper Thompson - 2026-09-23

Right - but that rule is problematic (in a patient-safety-level scale if a system actually uses patient compartment).

But for AppointmentResponse specifically:

If the patient is the one responding, then that AppointmentResponse will be in the compartment.
If the provider is responding, then it won't be. But they'd be on the Appointment.participant list, so that's fine. And our view is that the provider AppointmentResponse to an Appointment with the patient doesn't need to be in the compartment.
For Appointments about the patient where the patient is not a participant, that may be a problem. But that is about Appointment, not AppointmentResponse.

### 5. Lloyd McKenzie - 2026-09-23

The general rule is that if something is tied to a patient, there should be a direct link to Patient on it. We don't do 'inheritance'. The only exception there is Binary - for obvious reasons.

### 12. Lloyd McKenzie - 2026-05-12

If the focus is group, that means they belong to the Group compartment, not the Patient compartment (as would happen in social services apps and in certain other countries). Unless you're dealing with veterinary or certain public health scenarios, Group wouldn't be expected to ever be the 'subject' in the U.S. (or Canada or most other western countries) for that very reason. Instead you'd pick the Patient who the record would be tied to and then specify a 'focus' of the Group.

- **Resolution Description**: Specialty: The type/area of healthcare that the practitioner provides (e.g. Pediatrics, Psychiatry)

Code: The specific role (e.g. Pediatrician, Physician, Psychologist)

The term "authorized" might be a little strong for what was intended here. It could have been a more subtle term like decided, allocated, approved, agreed, arranged or other similar terms.

Change:
Roles which this practitioner is authorized to perform for the organization

to: (removing the un-intended nuance of authorization)
Roles which this practitioner can perform for the organization. Some specific roles may have associated qualifications neccesary.

Change the Requirements from:
Need to know what authority the practitioner has - what can they do?

to:
Need to know what activity(s) the practitioner is allocated/assigned for the organization - what can they do?

Also change comments:
A person may have more than one role.

to:

An individual may have more than one role.

Change the PractitionerRole.specialty definition from:
The specialty of a practitioner that describes the functional role they are practicing at a given organization or location.

to

The specialty of a practitioner describes the functional area of expertise they are practicing at a given organization or location.

Change the PractitioenrRole.specialty comments from:
The specialty represents the functional role a practitioner is playing within an organization/location. This role may require the individual have certain qualifications, which would be represented in the Practitioner.qualifications property. Note that qualifications may or might not relate to or be required by the practicing specialty.

to:
The specialty represents the functional area of expertise a practitioner is playing within an organization/location. Some specific specialties may have associated qualifications neccesary.

Also add to the Boundaries and Relationships section additional text covering that some roles/specialties require qualifications:

Qualifications (from the Practitioner resource) do not imply a Role, but might be considered when an Organization allocates practitioners to roles within their organization, and could provide useful information (such as expiry information) which could need to be tracked in some situations to ensure they continue to be eligible for a specific role.

to:
Qualifications (from the Practitioner resource) do not imply a role, but for some roles and/or specialties a qualification might be relevant/required, and could provide useful information (such as expiry information) which could need to be tracked in some situations to ensure they continue to be eligible.

- **Description**: Although PractitionerRole.code and .specialty both contain codes for roles at an organization, they are intended to cover different dimensions. However, the current definitions do not make those intended dimensions / differences very clear.

The current descriptions highlight that .code is for roles this practitioner is authorized to perform, while .specialty is for functional roles they are practicing. It also notes that .specialty might require qualifications.

It was probably not intended to imply that .code roles are authorized, but not functional, not practicing, and not requiring qualifications. Or that .specialty roles are functional, practicing and qualified, but not authorized.

- **Description**: It is unclear how to record certain activity involving biologically derived products.

At the moment, "application" of a BDP, say a blood transfusion, or organ transplant, is recorded with a Procedure. This is analogous to how implantation of how an artificial hip (Device) is recorded. Although appropriate for some situations, it may or may not be appropriate for other use cases.

For example, how would a patient assertion of "I had a blood transfusion 20 years ago" be recorded? Is the preferred way to create a Procedure? Is this an Observation? Perhaps (in this case) we would prefer that it be a MedicationStatement. (Note that in most cases, a BDP resource representing the actual product wouldn't be available, only a code representing the type of BDP.)

Right now BDP requests are tracked through ServiceRequest. Is there need for a separate resource? Is this a special kind of MedicationRequest? Or is ServiceRequest sufficient?

Is Procedure sufficient for a historical BDP-use statement, or is there a need for a BDPStatement?

And finally, there doesn't appear to be a distinction between a Procedure and "administration" of a BDP; this may align with how BDPs are used or may be an oversight.

Please review the existing resources for these various BDP uses, and determine if changes are needed to the preferred existing resources or if new resources are needed. For example, if MedicationStatement is the preferred way of recording "I had a blood transfusion", then should MedicationStatement.medication allow reference to BDP?

### 6. David Barwin - 2025-06-26

I'm personally comfortable using ServiceRequest as the requesting resource and Procedure to record administration details for BiologicallyDerivedProduct (BDP). While there is some potential benefit in applying something like the MedicationAdministration.dosage concept for certain classes of BDP, this is not a universally applicable requirement due to the highly generalized nature of the BDP resource. BDPs cover a wide spectrum from blood and tissue products to future concepts like xeno-derived products. That said, if there's an established resource pattern intended for these use cases, it does not appear to be currently followed.

I also agree that there's a gap on historical use scenarios especially for BDPs related to stem cell transplants, where the relevance of such events may extend across a patient’s lifetime. Retention requirements for this type of data often exceed 20 years, and in some jurisdictions (e.g. for pediatric patients) may stretch to 100 years. It is currently difficult to record the after-the-fact history using FHIR terminology.

The key challenge in these historical scenarios is that specific instances of BDPs are often not identifiable due to data availability limitations. Additionally, the BDP is typically a small part of a much broader clinical process. For example, a patient treated for acute myeloid leukemia with a myeloablative hematopoietic stem cell transplant would have received a BDP as part of that treatment. However, their medical history would also include dozens of related procedures from chemotherapy and radiation to infection control measures such as dental care, all of which are clinically significant in context. The patient would likely report that they had chemotherapy and a stem cell transplant without the level of detail necessary to do actual resource references.

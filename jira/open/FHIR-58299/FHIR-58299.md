Condition
recorder
0..1
Reference(Practitioner | PractitionerRole | Patient | RelatedPerson | Group)
Individual who recorded the record and takes responsibility for accurately recording its content.
Because the recorder takes responsibility for accurately recording information in the record, the recorder is the most recent author. The recorder might or might not be the asserter. By contrast, the recordedDate is when the condition was first recorded.

Procedure
recorder
0..1
Reference(Patient | RelatedPerson | Practitioner | PractitionerRole)
Individual who recorded the record and takes responsibility for its content.
The recorder takes responsibility for the recorded content. The source from where they got the information can be captured using reportedReference. If you want to capture the scribe, that's typically handled with Provenance.

Individual who recorded the record and takes responsibility for accurately recording its content.
Because the recorder takes responsibility for accurately recording information in the record, the recorder is the most recent author. The recorder might or might not be the asserter. By contrast, the recordedDate is when the allergy or intolerance was first recorded.

## Comments

### 1. Michelle Miller - 2026-09-17

LisaRNelson Have you reviewed all the old/prior Group-related JIRAs? This has been discussed quite broadly across FHIR-I and several other work groups over the past few years when GrahameGrieve logged several of the JIRAs asking for Group to be added to meet the use case in Africa where care is very often provided to and administered for families, not patients, and families are considered to act as a group.

For example, FHIR-41403 has a comment from Grahame saying
both the recorder and asserter could be say, from a questionnaire that the family filled in
The past discussions (broadly) about Group resulted in this being added to the Group resource (Group - FHIR v6.0.0-snapshot1)
NOTE: Group may be used to define families or households, which in some circumstances may act collectively or have a degree of legal or formal recognition. This should be considered an exception. When Group is used for types of entities other than Patient or RelatedPerson, the expectation remains that the Group will not act collectively or have formal recognition - use Organization if these behaviors are needed.

For example, it is possible for a 'family' Group to be a performer of an Observation or owner of a Task. However, this is not permitted for a Group made up of Practitioners, PractitionerRoles or Organizations. Organization or CareTeam would need to be used instead. A Group of Practitioners could, however, be a subject of an Observation.
A couple of the historical FHIR-I (Group) discussions were part of FHIR-47603, FHIR-49851, FHIR-50525 etc.

### 2. Lisa R. Nelson - 2026-09-17

I couldn't figure out how to edit my comment and I am noticing typos, So I'm fixing them here:

michelle.m.miller I don't think a Group can record anything. Unless the system being used offers a "shared document" type of technology like Google docs, where multiple people can write into one object at the same time. I just can't imagine there are situations where one Condition can be recorded by multiple individuals. The role we are describing is "Recorder" and you need to think through what that implies. Same for Organization. By definition, an Organization is not an Individual. You can't put an Organization into a role that inherently can only be performed by an Individual.

This is a very FUNDAMENTAL principle that should be discussed more broadly to make sure everyone is on the same page here. Seems like more of a supreme court level decision which should not be left to the lower courts. I also added a lengthy comment in the other ticket related to this one, FHIR-58401. Big questions here, not small ones.

- **Description**: Discussions in PC and OO have highlighted useful distinctions between when condition information is represented in a Condition resource and when it is represented in an Observation resource. The following edits to Section 9.2 under Condition are proposed:

9.2 Resource Condition – Content
A clinical condition, problem, diagnosis, or other event, situation, issue, or clinical concept that has risen to a level of concern that may warrant tracking and/or management over time.

9.2.1 Scope and Usage
This resource is used to record detailed information about a condition, problem, diagnosis, or other event, situation, issue, or clinical concept that has risen to a level of concern and is assigned an updateable clinical status. A Condition resource typically spans a period of time and is revised to remain current. The cCondition could be created or updateda point in time diagnosis in the context of an encounter to reflect the current diagnostic assessment, it could be an item on the practitioner’s Problem List, or it could be a concern that doesn’t exist on the practitioner’s Problem List.

9.2.2 Boundaries and Relationships
The cCondition resource may be referenced by other resources as "reasons" for an action (e.g. MedicationRequest, Procedure, ServiceRequest, etc.)
This resource is not typically used to record information about subjective and objective information that might lead to the recording of a Condition resource. Such point-in-time signs and symptoms are typically captured using the Observation resource; although in some cases a persistent symptom, e.g. fever, headache may be captured as a cCondition before a definitive diagnosis has beencan be discerned by a clinician. In an inpatient scenario, a nursing problem list may document symptoms (such as respiratory alteration) as cConditions if they are the focus of care provision. It became a problem because the nurse (clinician) wants to manage it. By contrast, headache may be captured as an Observation when it contributes to the establishment of a meningitis Condition.
Use the Observation resource when a symptom is resolved without long term management, tracking, or when a symptom contributes to the establishment of a condition.
Use the Condition resource when a symptom requires long term management, tracking, or is used as a proxy for a diagnosis or problem that is not yet determined.
The assessment by a radiologist in an imaging DiagnosticReport of the presence or absence of a particular condition is recorded as a point-in-time observation of the presence of the condition. Such an Observation resource might or might not trigger the creation or revision of a Condition resource for tracking that condition over time.
Note that a Condition represents an instance of a condition, not the categorical patient state. This can be a subtle distinction for systemic conditions, but it is easier to see with conditions that can happen more than once, e.g. refuting one record of a wound does not mean that the patient does not have any other wounds, and resolving one case of otitis media does not rule out recurrence. An oObservation that the patient doesn't have any wounds means the patient doesn't have any wounds at that point in time.

### 4. Michelle Miller - 2023-08-17

FHIR Conference Call on 8/17/2023

Jay's proposed draft:

A foundational concept is the problem. Healthcare is developing better tactics for promoting healthy lifestyles, but the most urgent issues [that providers can address] are problems, and the most intuitive way to structure the patient record is around problems. In a problem-oriented record, specialists can confer on specific views of the patient without being distracted by irrelevant details - as long as those details are not actually relevant after all. [cite Weed?]

FHIR supports five resources that facilitate the problem-oriented view: Observation, ClinicalImpression, Condition, List, and CarePlan.

Observations are assertions of phenomena at points in time. They may represent all that is known about a patient, but they are not inherently organized, and they require other tools for effective use.

A ClinicalImpression pulls observations together to assess information and form a judgment. [Has anyone implemented this? Should we just move 'investigation' and 'prognosis' to Condition?]

A Condition is a record of some state of concern that will in most cases be tracked and treated. The distinction with Observation is not semantic but procedural: Observation and Condition may both record the presence of a cough, but the Observation is just the fact, whereas the Condition is the record used in managing clinical work. The status of an Observation is usually "final" when the act of observing is complete, whereas the status of a Condition reflects the patient's progress.

Two popular criteria for Condition are whether an item is tracked over time and whether it is of concern. Since "concern" is a matter for judgment, many people prefer the more objective "tracking" as heuristic, but note that Condition is used for encounter diagnoses, which may cease to be tracked after an encounter.

Not everything of concern is a condition: it's not uncommon to see a patient's Coronary Bypass Graft operation on a problem list. To support the gathering of items of concern in one place, FHIR defines the List resource. Using List allows different providers - and different kinds of providers - to have different problem lists for a patient. An EHR may maintain a master problem list, including all active Conditions and any other item any provider has added to a list.

The CarePlan resource allows providers to see not only problems and evidence in one place, but also goals and interventions, planned and completed. A system may leverage institutional knowledge such as care protocols and pathways to assist in the creation of these plans, and record items may be included based on knowledge-based algorithms or based on explicit direction from a provider. All CarePlan goals and interventions are organized by the Condition of concern that they address via a compositional design where plans for each Condition are contained in more general plans.

One area of ambiguity is the relationship between Condition and Problem list. While FHIR supports the use of List for a variety of use cases (a general problem list, provider- or specialty-focused lists, patient concern lists), some systems simply use active Conditions as the problem list. While this approach imposes limits, it is reasonable, and it is also not mutually exclusive with the List approach.

Discussion

Need to include how to connect the discrete pieces to visualize the pattern over time?

### 7. Jay Lyle - 2021-06-25

A draft:

Providers need to be able to understand what's important about a patient and how the various record artifacts relate to each other. This is important for any patient, but the more serious a patient's problems, the more data there is likely to exist in the record, and the more important it is to be able to navigate that data effectively.

- **Description**: Problem:

Goals representing ongoing patient behaviors — care plan compliance, medication adherence, self-management — are not naturally "met" once; they require indefinite periodic reassessment (e.g., reviewed every 3 months). Today, expressing this cadence structurally forces either:
(a) a single dueDate/dueDuration that must be manually reset each cycle, misrepresenting a recurring pattern as a series of one-time due dates, or
(b) defining the recurring schedule entirely outside Goal (e.g., a repeating Task or CarePlan.activity), with the pertainsToGoal extension providing a link back to the Goal but no way for the Goal itself to express how often it expects to be reviewed.

As a result, a goal's "ongoing-ness" (Goal.continuous = true) and its actual review cadence live in two disconnected places — one on the Goal resource, one on whatever external workflow resource happens to implement the recurrence — with no spec-defined way to tie them together within Goal itself.

Proposed change:

Add Timing (this is a resource structural change) as a third choice type for Goal.target.due[x] (i.e., dueDate | dueDuration | dueTiming), allowing a target to express a recurring review cadence (e.g., Timing.repeat.frequency/period/periodUnit) rather than only a single point or offset.
Revise Goal.target's definition and comment to acknowledge two target patterns:
(a) a measurable checkpoint to be achieved by/within a due date (current framing, unchanged), and
(b) a recurring review point for an ongoing goal, where dueTiming indicates reassessment cadence rather than a completion deadline - (the expectation is this will be used together with Goal.continuous = true, and reassessed against Goal.achievementStatus / achievementStatusDate at each cycle rather than resolved to achieved).

Suggest this revised comment: "When multiple targets are present for a single goal instance, all targets must be met (or, for continuous goals, kept under ongoing review) for the goal to be considered on track."

Rationale / use case:
Goal-directed and patient-centered care models (geriatrics, chronic disease management, behavioral health) commonly define goals — like care plan compliance — as sustained behaviors rather than ending points. Goal.continuous already signals this intent at the resource level, but target has no matching mechanism to express how often that ongoing behavior should be reassessed. The pertainsToGoal extension solves resource-to-goal linkage but not schedule expression, so implementers are still forced into workarounds — repeatedly updating a single dueDate, or pushing all cadence logic into external Task/CarePlan resources with no representation on Goal itself.

Backward compatibility:
- Existing dueDate / dueDuration usage and the current one-time-completion interpretation of target remain unchanged.
- dueTiming is a new option, not a replacement
- No impact to pertainsToGoal or existing linkage patterns

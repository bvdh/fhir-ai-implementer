- **Resolution Description**: Will remove the ISO definition of "EHR composition" and rewrite the opening paragraph to more generally convey the intended scope and usage of FHIR Compositions. Will keep the two top-level examples (FHIR Documents / Context-Establishing Snapshots) after further clarification of those terms.
- **Request in-person**: Hans Buitendijk
- **Description**: The introduction of the ISO/TR 12773 definition of EHR Composition is unnecessarily restricting the scope of a Composition, particularly that it "...binds a committed set of information to a subject,....". Unless a Composition is used as part of a FHIR Document, it need not be a committed set of information.

It seems that the essence of a Composition is that it is an organized data set or collection. A document need not only be organized, but has other characteristics as well that are conveyed through the Bundle it is part of or otherwise.

Therefore suggest to start with the core purpose of a Composition: organization. Where it is used as part of a FHIR Document, the variety of examples from ISO/TR 12773-2 is very helpful. But to be a Composition it need not represent any of those. Furthermore, it is unclear what "committed" actually means, while it should not meant that they are stored together in an EHR.

The term context-establishing snapshot is a reasonable synonym, while FHIR Document represent a subset of that. However, it is unclear what it means that a context-establishing snapshot is to be annotated and attested. So it seems that a Composition is an organized, context-establishing snapshot about the status of a subject/object. And where that is used to capture a document, the ISO-TR 12773-2 examples clarify what they may be.

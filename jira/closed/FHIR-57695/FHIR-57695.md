- **Resolution Description**: A Consent resource typically starts in the `draft` state while it is being prepared and reviewed when it is not yet intended to be acted upon.
Once the consent is executed and confirmed (through completion of the consent ceremony, such as signatures, verbal agreement, or implicit approval, depending on the workflow and consent type), it transitions to `active,` and becomes enforceable and effective in governing actions.
If the consent ceremony is never completed, the draft consent may be considered abandoned (based on policy) and its status may change to `not-done`.
If an active consent is later withdrawn or revoked by the patient (or an authorized representative) its status is updated to `inactive.`
The status of a consent created in error (e.g., wrong patient) is set to`entered-in-error`.
Consent expiry is handled implicitly via the `period` element rather than a dedicated status code; an active consent is considered expired once it is outside its valid timeframe and ceases to apply.
Changes to consent status are commonly managed either by leveraging FHIR versioning or by creating a successor Consent resource while marking the predecessor as inactive. A Provenance resources can be used to record an accounting of the status transitions and the reasons for such changes.

The sentence above is problematic.

### 4. Rebecca Baker - 2026-09-17

The use case this particular FHIR element is for is patient care and related to risk assessments that are documented as Incident Reports. For example, if a wrong medication was almost given, or if a wrong site surgery was almost performed, an Incident Report is documented so that the Quality teams (Quality and Risk Management) can assess what happened and put processes in place. My understanding is the statement highlighted is for general guidance to prevent documentation of allergies on the Adverse Event resource. I agree with the statement that "An adverse drug reaction can be allergic/immunologic, but it can also be a predictable pharmacologic effect or another harmful reaction. For example, bleeding from an anticoagulant is an adverse reaction but generally isn't an "allergy" to the anticoagulant." However, a healthcare professional would likely not document this as such.

It is important to see the entire contextual statement to comprehend the sentence: "It would be unusual for a recorder of an adverse event to not know this value; unknown code is included to support legacy data and is strongly discouraged. AllergyIntolerance should be used for the initial capture or recording of the individual's propensity to an adverse reaction to a substance. If an AllergyIntolerance does not exist, then an adverse reaction should be recorded as an AllergyIntolerance. If an AllergyIntolerance does exist and the substance was given, then an adverse reaction should be recorded as an AdverseEvent due to the aberrant workflow."

In the Vulcan AE IG, only "actual" adverse events are recorded: https://hl7.org/fhir/uv/ae-research-ig/2023Sep/StructureDefinition-AdverseEvent-clinical-research.html Thus, the definition of the adverse event is defined by ICH:

Adverse Event (or Adverse Experience)
Any untoward medical occurrence in a patient or clinical investigation
subject administered a pharmaceutical product and which does not
necessarily have to have a causal relationship with this treatment.
An adverse event (AE) can therefore be any unfavourable and unintended sign
(including an abnormal laboratory finding, for example), symptom, or disease
temporally associated with the use of a medicinal product, whether or not
considered related to the medicinal product.

https://database.ich.org/sites/default/files/E2A_Guideline.pdf

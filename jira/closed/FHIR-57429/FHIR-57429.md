- **Resolution Description**: `Medication.instance` aligns Medication with the [Product pattern](product.html), which has `Product.instance` with `identifier`, `lotNumber` and expiry for physical instances of a product. Both identifiers are kept, with different purposes:

`Medication.identifier` is the business identifier of the Medication record, for example a catalogue, formulary or stock-system number. It applies whether the Medication represents a kind of medication or a physical instance.
`Medication.instance.identifier` is the unique identifier of the physical item, typically a serial number (for example, from a GS1 DataMatrix). It can differ from the business identifier of the record.
`instance.identifier` is not used for batch or lot numbers. These go in `Medication.instance.lotNumber`.
`Medication.code` identifies the kind of medication, whether or not the Medication represents an instance.

We will update:

*Medication.identifier* comment: replace "The serial number could be included as an identifier." with "This is the business identifier of the Medication record. It is particularly important when the Medication represents a kind of medication, including when it is used as a canonical resource (with url and version), so that the medication can be identified and referenced across systems. For a Medication that represents a physical instance, the business identifier is less relevant: the serial number of the item is conveyed in Medication.instance.identifier, and the batch or lot number in Medication.instance.lotNumber."
*Medication.instance.identifier* comment: "The unique identifier of the physical item, typically a serial number, following the Product pattern. This may differ from the business identifier of the Medication record (Medication.identifier). It is not used for batch or lot numbers, which are conveyed in Medication.instance.lotNumber."
*Examples:* add a pair of Medication examples for the same product: one representing the kind (`code` and `identifier`, and `url` and `version` once FHIR-57813(https://jira.hl7.org/browse/FHIR-57813) is applied; no `instance`) and one representing a physical instance (`code`, its own `identifier`, and `instance.identifier`, `instance.lotNumber` and `instance.expirationDate`), showing the different use of the identifiers.

Notes:

The current examples med0303 and med0306 already use `instance.identifier` for a serial number and a GS1 string, but none has a top-level `Medication.identifier`.
Related: FHIR-57813(https://jira.hl7.org/browse/FHIR-57813) (Persuasive, resolved 2026-07-06) makes Medication optionally a canonical resource, adding `url`, `version`, `name`, `title` and the other CanonicalResource elements. A Medication representing a kind can then be identified by `url`/`version` and `identifier`; for an instance, the business identifier is less relevant.
This disposition should be applied together with FHIR-57813, or after it.

- **Description**: What is the role of Medication.identifier when the Medication represents an instance?

If it doesn't apply to the instance, then that needs to be clear.
If it does apply to the instance, then we don't need Medication.instance.identifier.

Side issue - what is the relationship between Medication.identifier and Medication.code, in the case where this is an instance? Do these properties change in this mode, or are they not to be used?

In draft R6 Medication.batch has been renamed to Medication.instance (which is an unwelcome breaking change).

Any renaming change at this time needs to be a real improvement, to justify breaking existing implementations.

The reason for renaming seems to be around adding Medication.instance.identifier. But it is not clear what that is for - given that Medication.identifier exists, and presumably identifies this object.

Is the instance backbone meant to act as a flag to say "yes this is an instance"?
That is somewhat clunky, but if so then maybe Medication.identifier should say not to use it when Medication.instance is used.

It is notable that there are now 3 places where a batch identifier could go. It was confusing enough when there were only 2.

My take on all this is:
Medication can be an instance or a definition.
That is conceptually awkward but actually not a problem in reality.
Software does not really need to decide which it is.
Usually the software will just get the data that it needs and all will be fine (if it's a request, then its never an instance, but if its a dispense, then it will be, and it can have a batch number).

However when it is an instance there seems no reason not to put instance level identifiers in Resource.instance. What else would go there?

I suggest .instance backbone is renamed back to .batch and we use Medication.identifier for instance level identifiers.

I have also never understood why lotNumber is needed when that is really just an identifier. But I expect the rationale is that some strings would not be allowed as identifiers. Documentation for that needs to make it clear why you use it rather than identifier.

Although it looks nice to have a batch (or "instance") backbone element to isolate and draw attention to the parts to use in instances, the current duplication actually complicates the use and overall understandability of the resource.

It would be better to just document fully (rather than superficially with a backbone name), and not duplicate anything.

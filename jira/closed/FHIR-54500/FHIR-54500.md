- **Resolution Description**: Background:

To clarify, there is no FamilyHistory section in the IPS Composition profile. So the tracker title is hypothetical.

However, the Composition documents using a sub-section per family member, each with its own section.focus.

Refer to the Composition.section.focus comment:
"Typically, sections in a document are about the subject of the document... For some kind of documents, some sections actually contain data about related entities. Typical examples are a section in a newborn discharge summary concerning the mother, or family history documents, with a section about each family member.
{} The reporter calls this approach "overkill" and instead proposes wrapping family-member information in Observation, obliquely referencing "each resource referenced from within a Composition expresses its own individual context."

(Note the FamilyMemberHistory Resource subject is the patient)

The reporter's complaint is partially valid

This is a really a documentation gap.

The "Compositions about multiple entities" section in does not address the family-history use case explicitly, despite family history being a well-known CDA pattern that FHIR implementers expect guidance on.
The spec's recommendation (sub-section per family member, found only in the section.focus element comment) is not surfaced in the place readers would look for it (Section 2.8.7).

Recommendation:

Add a paragraph to Section 2.8.7 ("Compositions about multiple entities") that surfaces the family-history pattern recommendation from the Composition.section.focus element comment. Suggested:
"For documents that contain information about multiple related entities — such as a family history document with information about multiple family members, or a newborn discharge summary referencing the mother — the recommended pattern is a sub-section per related entity, each with its own Composition.section.focus pointing at the relevant RelatedPerson or other resource. This pattern makes each related entity's information independently locatable, attestable, and queryable."

- **Description**: In all these cases, the subject of the document is a single patient, but some of the details are actually related to other persons or entities. When this happens, these other entities are detailed in the Composition.section.focus element. In the absence of a focus, it is assumed that the subject of the composition is the focus.

If a focus is specified, then the resources referred to from the section SHALL either:

specify that their subject (however named e.g. patient) or focus element (if present) is the focus indicated
not have a subject element

A few compositions genuinely cover multiple subjects - different sections are about different subjects. In such case, Composition.subject is omitted, and the extension section-subject is used on each section to indicate the subject.
In the case of a family history section, it might refer to resources that have as a subject one of the familiy members. This section requires a sub-section for each family member which is overkill. What is the recommendation for such information? Wrap the information in Observations?

Please clarify.

## Comments

### 1. Gay Dolin - 2026-05-29

We should review and possibly revise "When this happens, these other entities are detailed in the Composition.section.focus element. In the absence of a focus, it is assumed that the subject of the composition is the focus". Rationale is that these statements do not align with the FHIR FamilyMemberHistory resource, which does NOT use subject or focus, but rather, FamilyMemberHistory.relationship. There is no need for nested sections or altering subjects or even specifying focus if we use FamilyMemberHistory instances in a Composition's Family History section.

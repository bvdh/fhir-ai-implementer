- **Resolution Description**: We agree with the concern about having two different rule models in Consent and Permission. This is more a matter of how these two concepts historically have come to exist in the specs at different stages of FHIR.

Radical changes such as removing provisions from consent or combining Permission and Consent have been proposed before and are appealing from an engineering perspective but the reality is that we also need to balance these concerts with the concerns about maintaining continuity and breaking changes.

There are currently other proposed dispositions to enable pointing to a Permission resource from the consent in order to computably articulate the consent rules (in lieu of using provisions).

https://jira.hl7.org/browse/FHIR-53535

This will address the main concern in this ticket and will enable implementers and profilers to choose this approach an unify their rules language to that of the Permission resource which is general enough to encompass both jurisdictional and consent rules.

We will add discussion to the narrative to also elaborate on this.

- **Description**: In reviewing the FHIR R4-based Consent Management IG and the FHIR R6 direction with Permission, we suggest that a fundamental problem needs to be addressed where data sharing rules by patients (consents), jurisdictions (permissions or consents?), and organizations (permissions) are being managed differently, yet are at the core the same rules on what data can be shared or not. These rules in combination will determine whether a data source can or cannot share the data.

One approach to remove these challenges is to expand the scope of Consent to encompass Permissions and align the .policyBasis and .provision constructs to enable expression of the relevant rules by the grantor (jurisdiction/organization and patient in particular) by focusing the .policyBasis.reference on Consent only and including a uri within provision enabling references to a computable expression in a library for common, standard consents. Noting that Permissions has additional attributes (e.g., expanded action-activity attributes), these seem to reasonably can be added to Consent, thus promoting consistency in the sharing of data sharing rules

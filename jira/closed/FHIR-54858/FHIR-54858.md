Note: EN 13606 icon uses the term "Composition" to refer to a single commit to an EHR system, and offers some common examples: a composition containing a consultation note, a progress note, a report or a letter, an investigation report, a prescription form or a set of bedside nursing observations. Using Composition for an attested EHR commit is a valid use of the Composition resource, but for FHIR purposes, it would be usual to make more granular updates with individual provenance statements.

to:

Scope and Usage
ISO defines the term EHR composition as a "set of information committed to one EHR by one agent, as a result of a single clinical encounter or record documentation" and offers these examples: Progress Note, radiology report, referral letter, clinic visit record, discharge summary, functional health assessment, diabetes review.[^1] FHIR adopts this concept as the Composition resource — the structural unit that binds a committed set of information to a subject, authors, and context, along with the attested narrative that represents the information. Where ISO frames an EHR composition as the product of one encounter or documentation event, FHIR carries the same structure into two use cases that differ in how they are packaged and how long they persist:

FHIR Documents — attestable, immutable Bundles with composed narrative.
Context-Establishing Snapshots — an annotated, attested list of queryable FHIR resources that represents the current status of the subject.

Using Composition for FHIR Documents

A Composition is the basic structure from which FHIR Documents - immutable bundles with attested narrative - are built. Composition defines the structure and narrative content necessary for a document. However, a Composition alone does not constitute a document. Rather, the Composition is the first entry in a Bundle where Bundle.type=document, and any other resources referenced from Composition must be included as subsequent entries in the Bundle (for example, Patient, Practitioner, Encounter, etc.). A single logical composition may be associated with a series of derived documents, each of which is a frozen copy of the composition. See the FHIR Documents section for more information on the document structure, presentation, and processing.

The Clinical Document profile constrains Composition to specify a clinical document (matching CDA). See also the comparison with CDA.
Using Composition for Context-Establishing Snapshots

The Composition can be used to record the current status of the subject. This use of Composition supports persistence and clinical attestation beyond simple enumeration. The sections establish the relationship of the entries within the overall context and provide information about the relevant parties involved in assembling the information. Unlike FHIR Documents, this use of Composition serves as the structural backbone for non-document artifacts and changes as the subject's status changes.
[^1]: ISO/TR 12773-2:2009(en) Business requirements for health summary records — Part 2: Environmental scan

2.8.2 Boundaries and Relationships

Composition is a structure for grouping information for purposes of persistence and attestability. The Composition resource defines a set of healthcare-related information that is assembled together into a single logical document that provides a single coherent statement of meaning, establishes its own context and that has clinical attestation with regard to who is making the statement. The Composition resource provides the basic structure of a FHIR document. The full content of the document is expressed using a Bundle containing the Composition and its entries.

There are several other grouping structures in FHIR with distinct purposes:

The List resource provides a flat enumeration of resources with per-entry flags (added, removed, deleted) and an optional snapshot/working mode. Composition differs from List in providing annotation and section structure: titled and coded sections, per-section narrative, per-section authorship, and document-level attestation. Use List for flat collections (dynamic or snapshot) where per-entry tracking is the primary need; use Composition for annotated, structured collections (typically packaged as a FHIR document) where sections, narrative, and attestation matter."

- **Description**: There are use cases where the composition is not only used as the basis for documents but rather for context-establishing snapshots of a connected web of resources. The sections establish the relationship of the entries within the greater context whereas the section authors give information about the relevant parties that were involved in putting the information together.

Such use cases are currently not covered by the description of the scope of the resource.

Proposal: Extend the Scope Section of the Composition to include use cases other than frozen in time documents.

For the inclusion of the described use case the relationships and boundaries section then needs to clarify the differences to the list resource:
The description correctly positions List as a mechanism for managing a flat, curated collection of resources that is typically dynamic from a business-process perspective.

There are, however, use cases where a time-specific, attestable snapshot is required that establishes its own clinical and contextual meaning as a coherent statement. In such cases, characteristics of Composition become relevant.

In particular, sections enable semantic structuring of content, and the document context supports persistence and clinical attestation beyond simple enumeration.

Consider clarifying in the text that Composition is intended for context-establishing snapshots, whereas List is optimized for dynamic collections and collection management.

### 11. Eric Haas - 2026-05-29

Consideration for persuasive disposition:
Proposed Disposition for FHIR-54858

Resolution: Persuasive with Modification Change Category: Correction (clarification of existing structural capability) Change Impact: Non-substantive (narrative-only update; no change to the Composition StructureDefinition, conformance behavior, or existing instances)
Background

The reporter, supported by four co-balloters, observes that Section 2.8.1 (Scope and Usage) of the Composition resource frames Composition exclusively as the basis for FHIR Documents — "immutable bundles with attested narrative" — and does not acknowledge use cases where Composition serves as the structural backbone for non-document artifacts. The reporter describes such use cases as "context-establishing snapshots of a connected web of resources" in which sections establish relationships among entries and section-level authorship records the parties involved in assembling the information.

Restated for triage clarity: the reporter is asking the Composition spec to acknowledge a use case where Composition serves as an annotated, structured table of contents for a point-in-time collection of related resources — with sections providing titled and coded grouping, narrative giving human-readable context, attestation supporting persistence and accountability, and per-section authorship recording who contributed each grouping. The alternative resources — Bundle (transport-only) and List (flat collection with per-entry flags) — lack the annotation, section-structure, and attestation layers Composition provides.
Existing precedent for the pattern

This pattern is not speculative. It is already deployed in published HL7 international IGs that depend on Composition for non-document purposes:

Order Catalog IG (HL7 Orders and Observations WG, v1.0.0, STU 2). The IG defines two Composition profiles — Catalog and CatalogHeader — used as the structural backbone for catalogs of laboratory services, medications, and medical devices.
The current Scope text creates a documentation gap

The current Scope and Usage section are inconsistent with this use case:

"A Composition is the basic structure from which FHIR Documents — immutable bundles with attested narrative — are built." — Restricts Composition scope to FHIR Documents.

"Using Composition for an attested EHR commit is a valid use of the Composition resource, but for FHIR purposes, it would be usual to make more granular updates with individual provenance statements." — actively discourages the snapshot pattern that the Order Catalog IG and the reporter both depend on, and it is unclear how it would work with Provenance.

The Boundaries and Relationships section distinguishes Composition from List on the basis of List's dynamism ("the notion of 'list' is dynamic – items are added and removed over time"), but does not state what Composition's positive differentiator is when both resources could carry a point-in-time collection. The differentiator that current text omits is annotation and section structure, not dynamism.

Recommendation

This disposition would resolve the tracker through narrative clarification rather than scope expansion. The Composition StructureDefinition already structurally supports the pattern the reporter is requesting — the gap is in the narrative description of what Composition is for.

1. Revise the Scope and Usage to acknowledge both use patterns.

Suggested replacement for the first paragraph:
"A Composition provides the structural backbone for two related artifact patterns:

FHIR Documents — attested, frozen Bundles with composed narrative, produced by the $document operation and transmitted between systems (see FHIR Documents).
{}Annotated point-in-time captures{*} — server-resident Composition resources used as an annotated, structured table of contents for a snapshot of related resources.

*Both patterns rely on the same Composition structural features: sections, narrative, defined subject/category/author, status lifecycle, and identifiable artifact identity. The distinction is one of packaging and lifecycle.
2. Remove the discouragement of server-resident attested compositions.

Replace the EN 13606 paragraph's "but for FHIR purposes, it would be usual to make more granular updates with individual provenance statements" clause to support a "documentation-session pattern" (where a Composition captures the assmbled information)

3. Update the Boundaries and Relationships section to clarify the List boundary on the right axis.

Replace the current List paragraph with text that distinguishes the two resources by annotation/structure rather than dynamism. Suggested replacement:
"List - provides a flat enumeration of resources with per-entry flags (added, removed, deleted) and an optional snapshot/working mode. Composition differs from List in providing annotation and section structure: titled and coded sections, per-section narrative, per-section authorship, and document-level attestation. Use List for flat collections (dynamic or snapshot) where per-entry tracking is the primary need; use Composition for annotated, structured collections (typically packaged as a FHIR document) where sections, narrative, and attestation matter."
4. Add an exempla of an Annotated point-in-time capture

Within Section 2.8.1, after the new dual-pattern framing, add an example demonstrating a Use Case
Open questions for the work group

Confirm the scope of "annotated point-in-time capture" with the reporter. AnnaLin's wording ("context-establishing snapshots") and the restatement above (annotated TOC) align on the structural pattern. Concrete examples from the reporter and co-balloters during the in-person discussion would confirm whether the proposed disposition language fits their actual implementation need. Specifically: does their use case have attestation? Per-section authorship? Is it tied to an Encounter or is it documentation-session-style without an encounter?

Coordination with FHIR Infrastructure WG. If the dual-pattern framing is accepted, the FHIR Documents page (owned by FHIR-I) should be updated to acknowledge that Composition has uses beyond document construction. The current documents.html framing is consistent with the document-only Scope text on Composition; both pages should move together to avoid creating a downstream inconsistency.

Coordination with Orders and Observations WG.

Confirm these changes align with the intent of the Lab Catalog IG.

Effect on related Composition trackers. Resolving FHIR-54858 with the dual-pattern framing has implications for:

FHIR-54505 (url vs. identifier) — strengthens the rationale for Composition.url as a canonical-reference target for server-resident captures, distinct from Composition.identifier as a business identifier.
FHIR-54509 (CanonicalResource implementation) — the case for declining CanonicalResource implementation weakens slightly, since annotated-capture Compositions (catalogs, reference datasets) are more reusable-artifact-like than clinical documents. The WG should revisit FHIR-54509 disposition after FHIR-54858 is resolved.
FHIR-54495 (DiagnosticReport boundary) — the Boundaries and Relationships section will already be open for editing; the DiagnosticReport boundary text from FHIR-54495's resolution should be added in the same editorial pass.

Coordination note

The in-person discussion that AjnaSchwab and AnnaLin specifically requested should be scheduled before the next ballot cycle to avoid risk of escalation to Negative votes on the next cycle.
